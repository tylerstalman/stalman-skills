## Video authenticity

### Never alter eye contact

- Never enable Eye Contact, eye correction, gaze correction, synthetic gaze, or an equivalent feature in any of Tyler's videos, in any editor, for any participant. Preserve the person's original eyes and natural gaze.
- Inspect inherited source edits, templates, duplicates, clips, participant tracks, and keyframes. Disable any existing eye-correction setting; preserving manual settings does not override this prohibition. Audio cleanup and Smart Layouts do not authorize gaze correction.
- Before export, scheduling, or publication, verify that no track, clip, or later keyframe enables eye correction. Inspect the latest rendered faces during playback as well; an absent setting alone does not establish that the source pixels are unaltered.
- If correction is baked into an imported render, return to the original camera recording. If it cannot be removed or verified, hold delivery/publication and report the gap instead of declaring the video ready.
- Correcting a saved edit does not replace an existing export or a published social post. Regenerate required deliverables and verify their revision; never delete or repost a live video without Tyler's instruction.

### Verify exact product names

- Before adding a product name, image, screenshot, 3D model, or specs, confirm the exact product, generation, and variant from the person's actual discussion and surrounding context. Check the original audio/picture when a transcript or partial name is ambiguous. Do not substitute a similar model, the newest generation, or a prior guess. If the discussion switches to an older product, time labels and visuals to that product rather than carrying the newer name across the anecdote.
- Verify the spelling against the manufacturer's official product page or documentation. Use its concise model name; include the brand only when needed for clarity. Preserve meaningful capitalization, lowercase suffixes, Roman numerals, numbers, spaces, and hyphens, including in graphics and captions. Do not let an all-uppercase style change a case-sensitive model suffix. Omit long marketing descriptions and keyword-heavy retailer titles.
- Apply that same verified short name consistently in hooks, product cards, dialogue-caption corrections, titles, descriptions, and publishing copy. If the exact identity remains unresolved, hold the specific label and report the uncertainty rather than guessing.
