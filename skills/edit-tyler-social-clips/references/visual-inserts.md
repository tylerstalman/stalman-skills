# Visual inserts

Read [camera-card production](camera-card-production.md) when building a camera card. Shared rules for every channel are in [visual production](../../short-form-video-core/references/visual-production.md).

## Animation timing and reading time

Time graphic entrances, spec reveals, and exits to the relevant spoken reference or editorial beat. Keep motion brief and purposeful, then hold the graphic still long enough to comfortably read every item at phone size. Count reading time after the entrance and final text reveal finish, before the exit begins; animation time is not reading time. Judge the hold from the actual copy and density rather than a fixed duration. If it feels rushed, extend the hold within the relevant passage or simplify/stagger the information. Check the playable timeline across cuts and review the entrance, readable hold, and exit in playback before calling the timing verified.

When chronology is the explanation, consider one evolving timeline rather than disconnected cards. Advance each milestone at the spoken reference, retain earlier milestones for context, and resume the same state after speaker cutaways instead of replaying its entrance. Verify dates and the precise event being dated; distinguish a specific manufacturer's product from a claim about the first product ever. Keep undated developments undated. Recompute word cues after dialogue or automated cleanup changes the playable timing.

## Screenshots

Place genuine screenshots on a plain **off-white canvas** (use the working brand value `#F4F0E6` unless Tyler supplies a newer value). The screenshot itself is the insert: do not add titles, labels, credits, specs, or other text outside the screenshot. Preserve text already within the original screenshot. Dialogue captions may overlap the screenshot; keep their approved style and do not shrink the screenshot just to reserve a caption band. Use the same external composition and rendered-video delivery workflow when needed for reliable Riverside playback. This screenshot treatment is distinct from the green paper camera-cutout cards below.

## Visual opportunity check before posting

Before publishing or scheduling any clip, review its actual discussion for clear opportunities to show a relevant screenshot or real product photo. Add visuals that help viewers recognize the specific app, product, feature, or example being discussed; do not force an insert into every clip. Prefer original screenshots and manufacturer photos, verify the exact product/version, and avoid presenting a generic image as the speakers' own work or the specific demo they describe. Time each insert to its spoken reference, allow comfortable viewing time, preserve caption styling, and check the return to speakers for stray frames. Follow the screenshot treatment above and the camera treatment below where applicable. If a useful source cannot be verified, report that gap before posting rather than substituting a guessed visual.

## Camera cutouts and basic specs

For future camera discussions in these short-form clips, use Tyler's paper-cutout treatment when introducing the camera:

- Confirm the exact model from the recording before choosing an image or specs; distinguish similar models and generations rather than guessing from a partial name. Verify the manufacturer's concise official model name and preserve its exact spelling/case in the rendered card. Recheck which generation is being discussed when the speaker moves to an older-camera anecdote.
- Find a real product photo, preferably from the manufacturer. Use an image-generation tool for a dark forest-green textured paper background and the photo cutout treatment: a slightly irregular white border and soft shadow, matching Tyler's Final Cut style. Keep the camera around 70–75% of frame width with breathing room. Typeset the specs in the brand's off-white. The approved working treatment used #F4F0E6; use an explicit current brand value if provided. Preserve the product's actual appearance; do not invent hardware or markings.
- Use the updated layout for future cameras: put **all model/spec text together at the top** and the **camera cutout in the lower portion**. Reserve a clear band for the actual dialogue captions and platform controls; the camera must sit clear of that band, rather than being pushed into it. Do not split specs between the top and bottom as in the first Ricoh card. Check the full animation paths as well as the resting layout so neither image nor spec text crosses the caption area. Tyler approved the Ricoh photo treatment; retain that visual style with this revised placement.
- Keep the card short and easy to scan: **model name, megapixels, lens focal length, weight, and release year**. Verify facts against manufacturer documentation or another authoritative source. Label 35mm-equivalent focal lengths clearly, include aperture when useful, and state whether weight includes the battery/card. Use the original model's release year, not a later variant's or firmware date.
- Use a brief, restrained camera entrance (such as a small zoom), a model-name reveal, and a light staggered spec reveal, followed by a readable still hold and simple exit. Follow the animation timing rule above; exact effects and durations are choices for the current passage, not fixed settings.
- Layer the insert over the relevant dialogue in the same edit, preserving the audio and overall clip length. Keep the hook, camera, specs, and dialogue captions from competing. Preserve Tyler's approved dialogue captions, including their white color, font, shadow, position, and animation. Style camera-spec text independently; adding a graphic does not authorize changing dialogue captions. If contrast is poor, adjust the graphic behind the captions rather than recoloring the captions.
- Compose the camera, paper, specs, and animations outside Riverside and render them into one finished vertical video insert before uploading. Do not rely on Riverside to generate or assemble this graphic: the image layer appeared in the desktop editor but was absent in Tyler's mobile Clips view. Retain the editable source artwork and typography locally, then insert the finished video over the existing dialogue.
- Verify actual saved placement, duration, layer order, and the return to the speakers. Check phone-size legibility and playback through the entrance, hold, and exit. Verify the photo survives in the playable export/app view; a desktop canvas screenshot alone does not establish delivery. Ensure any app export being reviewed reflects the latest edit revision.

Use the current editing tools and guide for upload, animation, and time-axis contracts. Do not reuse old media IDs, clip IDs, source timestamps, revisions, or camera-specific facts from an earlier episode.
