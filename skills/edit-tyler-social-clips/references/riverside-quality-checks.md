# Riverside cleanup and quality checks

## Audio cleanup and pacing

- For new clips, enable all five Riverside AI tools: Remove Pauses, Remove Filler Words, Magic Audio for every speaker, Smart Mute, and Smart Layouts with cutaways. These are part of the default workflow; do not leave them for Tyler to turn on manually. Favor stronger cleanup for short form. Exact strength labels and connector values remain to be verified; Tyler approved stronger cleanup without specifying an enum. These choices override different or unresolved full-episode settings.
- When revising an existing edit, inspect its latest state and preserve Tyler’s manual AI settings, cleanup, and layouts. Do not rerun cleanup or regenerate Smart Layouts merely to satisfy the defaults when Tyler has already configured them.
- After restoring or adding dialogue, verify that Riverside's automated **Remove Filler Words and Remove Pauses cover the current kept speech**. An old toggle or cuts on the previous selection do not prove that newly restored passages were processed. Run the missing automated pass using the established method/pace, then inspect the resulting cuts and mutes and listen to the affected joins. Preserve other manual settings and layouts. Filler removal follows transcript labels and can miss an audible filler; it is not a semantic judgment. Preserve meaningful uses of “like,” comparisons, qualifications and natural phrasing, and repair any missed or mistaken cleanup after listening.
- Review cleanup for clipped speech, unnatural joins, lost meaning, unwanted fragments, continuity, and synchronization.
- During crosstalk, ensure a filler or pause detected on one participant has not produced a global cut through another participant's words. Restore any damaged words and, where needed, mute only the intended participant instead. Recheck inherited mutes on restored dialogue even after an automated pass succeeds.

## Full review

Verify Eye Contact / eye correction is disabled for every participant throughout the edit, including inherited settings and later keyframes. Check natural eyes and gaze in the latest rendered playback. Do not call an edit ready when that verification is incomplete.

Check the opening establishes the subject cleanly and uses early visual or multicam opportunities; the edited story has a coherent beginning, middle, end and payoff; and the hook accurately describes the clip and follows the saved styling. In speaker-emphasis layouts, verify the main speaker occupies the large tile through relevant handoffs. Check camera variety, natural reactions, vertical framing, participant positions, audio continuity and sync. Report checks that could not actually be completed; inspecting settings alone is not a full audiovisual review.

Always double-check Riverside's generated edits rather than trusting Smart Layout or cleanup. Play the entire current clip and inspect suspicious shot changes frame by frame, especially brief shots lasting only a few frames, flashes of another angle, accidental layout changes, and tiny remnants around dialogue cuts or inserts. Use short scene durations as review flags, not automatic deletion criteria: preserve purposeful quick cuts. Correct clearly accidental flashes or stray shots by extending the appropriate neighboring shot or repairing the scene boundary, while preserving intended dialogue and sync. Replay each repaired boundary and verify the latest rendered version before calling the clip ready. If playback or frame inspection cannot be completed, explicitly report that the glitch-cut audit remains unverified.

Compute short-shot flags on the playable timeline after all cuts and overlays, not source duration alone. In particular, inspect the interval between an insert ending and the next speaker angle starting: an otherwise long source scene can collapse to a few-frame flash. Prefer a direct insert-to-speaker cut or an appropriate held shot over retaining that accidental fragment. Confirm adjacent frames on both sides of the repaired boundary. When a clip must update in Riverside's Clips feed, verify feed playback as well as the editor; a completed API render alone did not reliably establish that the app-facing version was refreshed in the observed workflow.
