## Topic discovery and selection

- Build a detailed, fairly long list of topics. Look at each topic's beginning as a promising entry point because it often gives new audiences context.
- Prefer interesting moments when a useful ranking method is available. A clear takeaway, surprise, useful advice, distinctive opinion, or engaging exchange are editorial heuristics, not reliable predictions of performance.
- For additional clips inspect existing clips when possible to avoid repeating selections.
- When Tyler identifies an episode, confirm each selected edit's source recording belongs to that episode. A shared studio or project is not sufficient because it can contain recordings from other episodes. Use other episodes only when Tyler authorizes a mixed batch.

## Opening and narrative

- Make the subject clear in the first few spoken seconds. Trim opening filler such as “so,” “like,” or “um” without clipping meaningful words.
- Aim for one to two minutes when the point fits clearly; material for a complete clip may span five minutes or more. These are estimates, not rigid limits. The shared core's coherence-before-compression review takes priority: retain setup and explanatory bridges, use a longer or more continuous passage when needed, and review the whole assembled script before release.
- Actively remove tangents, irrelevant detours, and repetition. Preserve a coherent beginning, middle, end and real takeaway, the speakers' intended meaning, necessary qualifications, and natural continuity.
- Seek opportunities for both speakers to contribute and respond when that improves the clip. Single-speaker clips are acceptable; don't force a second speaker.

## Opening text hook

Every clip opens with one short sentence of three to seven words about that clip, not the whole episode. Questions and statements are acceptable. Be descriptive, accurate, and explicit about the topic. Avoid clickbait, exaggeration, or promises the discussion does not fulfill.

Use **Staatliches**, black text on solid white rectangular strips, centered horizontally, with compact line spacing. Preserve the approved copy when adjusting placement/size. Uppercase styling must still preserve meaningful case-sensitive product suffixes. Riverside's Speaker preset is a starting point only; inspect its actual font, colors and box effect. Dialogue captions keep their independent approved style.

Keep the full text box clear of the platform header/search/top controls, right-side actions and bottom description/caption UI. Do not assume that keeping eyes clear also clears platform UI.

### Animation timing and reading time

Time graphic entrances, spec reveals, and exits to the relevant spoken reference or editorial beat. Keep motion brief and purposeful, then hold the graphic still long enough to comfortably read every item at phone size. Count reading time after the entrance and final text reveal finish, before the exit begins; animation time is not reading time. Judge the hold from the actual copy and density rather than a fixed duration. If it feels rushed, extend the hold within the relevant passage or simplify/stagger the information. Check the playable timeline across cuts and review the entrance, readable hold, and exit in playback before calling the timing verified.

## Review

Verify Eye Contact / eye correction is disabled for every participant throughout the edit, including inherited settings and later keyframes. Check natural eyes and gaze in the latest rendered playback. Do not call an edit ready when that verification is incomplete.

Check the opening establishes the subject cleanly and uses early visual or multicam opportunities; the edited story has a coherent beginning, middle, end and payoff; and the hook accurately describes the clip and follows the saved styling. In speaker-emphasis layouts, verify the main speaker occupies the large tile through relevant handoffs. Check camera variety, natural reactions, vertical framing, participant positions, audio continuity and sync. Report checks that could not actually be completed; inspecting settings alone is not a full audiovisual review.

Always double-check Riverside's generated edits rather than trusting Smart Layout or cleanup. Play the entire current clip and inspect suspicious shot changes frame by frame, especially brief shots lasting only a few frames, flashes of another angle, accidental layout changes, and tiny remnants around dialogue cuts or inserts. Use short scene durations as review flags, not automatic deletion criteria: preserve purposeful quick cuts. Correct clearly accidental flashes or stray shots by extending the appropriate neighboring shot or repairing the scene boundary, while preserving intended dialogue and sync. Replay each repaired boundary and verify the latest rendered version before calling the clip ready. If playback or frame inspection cannot be completed, explicitly report that the glitch-cut audit remains unverified.

Compute short-shot flags on the playable timeline after all cuts and overlays, not source duration alone. In particular, inspect the interval between an insert ending and the next speaker angle starting: an otherwise long source scene can collapse to a few-frame flash. Prefer a direct insert-to-speaker cut or an appropriate held shot over retaining that accidental fragment. Confirm adjacent frames on both sides of the repaired boundary. When a clip must update in Riverside's Clips feed, verify feed playback as well as the editor; a completed API render alone did not reliably establish that the app-facing version was refreshed in the observed workflow.
